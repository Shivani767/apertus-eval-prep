\subsection{Quantization, backend, and sampling move scores little}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Weight-only quantization changes scores by at most 2.87\,\pp{}
(Phi-3.5-mini INT4: 67.00\% to 69.88\%, raw $p=0.037$; not significant
after Holm, $p=0.736$). No quantization contrast survives family-wise
correction. Switching to vLLM on identical prompts moves SmolLM2-1.7B
by $+2.25$\,pp{} (raw $p=0.0036$; Holm $0.078$, BH $0.014$) and
Qwen2.5-3B by $+2.37$\,pp{} (raw $p=0.038$), with Phi-3.5-mini
unchanged ($+0.13$\,pp{}, $p=1.0$). The direction is consistent
(vLLM $\ge$ HF in all three cases) but no backend contrast is
significant after Holm correction. These small positive shifts are
consistent with backend numerical differences on generative tasks of
the kind documented for lm-eval \citep{biderman2024lessons}; at our
scale they are suggestions, not established effects.

Temperature-0.7 sampling (top-$p$ 0.95, three seeds) shifts net
accuracy by at most 3.5\,pp relative to greedy (SmolLM2-1.7B: 39.75\%
greedy vs.\ 36.25--39.00\% sampled; Qwen2.5-3B: 64.38\% greedy vs.\
63.00--65.00\% sampled), while per-item disagreement against the
control ranges from 9.9\% to 24.1\% across the six sampled contrasts.
Sampling noise is therefore a high-churn, low-drift process whose net
effect partially cancels. Under greedy decoding, changing the RNG seed
is an exact no-op: all six greedy-seed contrasts are item-for-item
identical to the control (0 discordant pairs out of 800 each), so
greedy seed variation contributes exactly zero measured variance in
this harness.

\subsection{Failure analysis}
\label{sec:failures}

The failure analysis covers the shared greedy matrix only, excluding
sampling and the single 7B cell. \cref{tab:failure-analysis} provides
post-hoc output categories across all successful paper runs. It contains
two outputs classified as unparseable in 19{,}200 scored item records
(one SmolLM2 concise cell, one Phi INT8 cell), and zero classified empty
outputs. These records reuse the same evaluation items across runs and are
not independent examples. Rare recorded parse failures do not establish
extractor validity: a plausible but incorrectly extracted answer may still
be classified as \emph{wrong\_answer}. Runtime failures absent from
completed-run artifacts cannot be counted from this analysis.

\paragraph{Failure categories.} Representative failure modes include
\textbf{format violations} (no extractable answer format produced),
\textbf{wrong extraction} (correct reasoning but wrong final answer),
\textbf{demonstration copying} (model echoes few-shot exemplars), and
\textbf{empty/near-empty outputs} (very rare). See \cref{tab:failure-analysis}
for examples and counts. The key observation is that parse failures are
negligible ($\leq$3 out of 24,800), but extraction errors within parseable
outputs are more common and may bias accuracy downward.

The single Qwen2.5-7B cell (INT4) scores 67.88\% (543/800; Wilson
[64.6, 71.0]). With no fp16 control for the 7B model we cannot
attribute anything about quantization at 7B, and we do not rank this
cell against other models.
